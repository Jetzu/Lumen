# Participant briefing

*Read aloud or hand over before the consent form. One page.*

Thank you for taking part. This session is about comparing two image-analysis programs,
**Fiji/ImageJ** and **Lumen**, on everyday microscopy tasks. We are testing the software, not you:
if something is hard to find or does not work the way you expect, that is exactly what we need to
learn, and it is the software's fault, not yours.

**What will happen.** The session takes about 75 minutes. You will do ten short tasks in one
program and then the same ten tasks in the other (half of the participants start with Fiji, half
with Lumen). Before each program we give you a two-minute tour. Each task comes on a card; I will
read it out and you can read it again any time. The tasks are things like opening an image,
adjusting its channels, thresholding and counting objects, blurring with a preview, tracking cells,
batch-processing a folder, and comparing two images.

**Thinking aloud.** Please say what you are thinking while you work: what you are looking for,
what you expect to happen, what surprises you. If you go quiet I will ask "what are you thinking?"
- that is a reminder, not a criticism.

**Help.** I will not tell you where things are. If you are stuck for five minutes on a task we will
move on, and I will show you one way to do it so that the next task is not blocked. You can skip a
task at any time by saying so.

**After each program** you will answer ten short questions about how usable you found it, and two
open questions.

**Recording.** We record the screen and your voice so that we can count what happened later. The
recording is identified by a number, not by your name, and is kept only for this study.

**You can stop at any time**, without giving a reason, and you can ask us to delete your data
afterwards.

Do you have any questions before we start?
